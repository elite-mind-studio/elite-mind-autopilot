The placebo effect: people got drunk on drinks with zero alcohol. Real study: in 2003 Seema Assefi and Maryanne Garry (published in Psychological Science) gave 148 students plain tonic water; half were told it was vodka and tonic. The tonic was poured from real vodka bottles and the glass rims were wiped with limes dipped in vodka. The 'vodka' group acted drunk, some showed physical signs of being drunk, and many insisted they had felt drunk when told the truth. They were also easier to mislead in a memory test, and more confident in their wrong memories. Lesson: what you believe changes how you act, so your beliefs about yourself (confidence, money, ability) work the same way.
