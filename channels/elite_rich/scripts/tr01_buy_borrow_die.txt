Why do billionaires borrow money when they already have billions? It's not because they need it. It's a tax trick. And Black is going to show you how it works.

Black owns one hundred million dollars in company stock. He wants ten million dollars to spend.

If he sells, he pays tax on the gain. His stock cost him almost nothing, so that's about two point four million dollars. Gone.

So Black doesn't sell. He walks into a bank and borrows the ten million, with his stock as the guarantee.

And here's the trick. A loan isn't income. So there's no income tax on it. Not one dollar.

Meanwhile, his stock keeps growing. And growth you haven't sold isn't taxed either. When the interest comes due, he just borrows again.

Then the final step. When Black dies, his kids inherit the stock, and the tax system resets its price to today's value. All those years of gains? Never taxed. Estate tax can still apply, but the gains are wiped clean.

Buy. Borrow. Die. That's what people call it.

In 2021, ProPublica reported that the twenty-five richest Americans gained four hundred and one billion dollars in wealth over five years, and paid just thirteen point six billion in federal income tax.
