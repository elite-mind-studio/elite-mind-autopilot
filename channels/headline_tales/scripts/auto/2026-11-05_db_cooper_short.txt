Have you ever heard of a man who hijacked a plane, jumped out with two hundred thousand dollars, and was never seen again? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

The twenty fourth of November, 1971. The day before Thanksgiving. A quiet man in a dark suit boards a flight from Portland to Seattle. He buys his ticket under the name Dan Cooper.

Soon after take off, he hands a flight attendant a note. He says he has a bomb.

His demands are simple. Two hundred thousand dollars in cash. And four parachutes.

In Seattle, he gets everything he asked for. He lets the passengers go. Then he orders the crew to take off again, and to fly south.

Somewhere over the dark forests of the Pacific Northwest, he opens the rear stairs of the plane. And he jumps. Into the night, with the money strapped to his body.

He is never seen again.

But here is the part that still drives investigators crazy.

The FBI searched for years. Soldiers walked the forests. Nothing. Even his famous name was a mistake. A reporter wrote it down as D. B. Cooper, and it stuck.

Then, in 1980, an eight year old boy named Brian Ingram is digging by the Columbia River. He finds bundles of old twenty dollar bills. Five thousand eight hundred dollars. It is part of Cooper's ransom.

It raised more questions than it answered.

In 2016, after forty five years, the FBI stopped actively investigating. To this day, it is the only unsolved hijacking in American commercial aviation history.

Did he die in the jump, or did he get away with it?
