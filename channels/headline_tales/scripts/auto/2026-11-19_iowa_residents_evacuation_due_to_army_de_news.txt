You won't believe this. The Army is set to detonate sixty-eight barrels—about five tons—of deteriorated lead azide in Middletown, Iowa, creating a plume of toxic lead dust.

Residents of the 360-person town were told to leave their homes Friday morning, and those who stay must seal windows and doors.

The explosions are planned for noon Central Time, and the Army says the dust could settle on farmland, playgrounds and even schools nearby.

To curb the cloud, officials will wet the soil, mist the air and surround the barrels with two-story sand barriers, but EPA monitoring will continue off-site.

Officials warn the lead particles are toxic to humans and animals, and they recommend washing cars and cleaning windowsills after the blast.
