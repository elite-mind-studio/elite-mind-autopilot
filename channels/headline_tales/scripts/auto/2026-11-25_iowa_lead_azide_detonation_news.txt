You won't believe this one. The U.S. Army blew up roughly five tons of unstable lead azide in Iowa, and models show a dust plume potentially reaching northeast Missouri.

The blast occurred at 9 a.m. Wednesday at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, using 68 barrels of the compound.

Army officials said mitigation steps—water saturation, sand barriers and foam—reduced particle spread, and EPA monitors recorded zero lead in the air within fifteen minutes.

Nonetheless, the Tuesday maps illustrate a shaded plume moving southwest toward the Missouri line, passing near Knox City and narrowing toward Macon by late afternoon.
