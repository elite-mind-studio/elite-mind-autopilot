You won't believe this. The U.S. Army detonated about five tons of lead azide in Iowa, creating a plume of toxic dust that could travel into Missouri. Could this dust affect farms and families?

The explosion occurred at nine a.m. Wednesday at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, using sixty-eight barrels that had stored the material for more than twenty years.

Maps show the plume moving southwest, reaching northeast Missouri by early afternoon and narrowing toward Macon by late afternoon, while wind arrows point the dust away from Burlington.

Officials warned residents within five miles southwest to evacuate and urged those up to twenty miles away to stay indoors, close windows, and cover livestock feed.

The USDA estimates two hundred fifty farms could be impacted, and cattle farmer Steve Lucie worries about soil and herd health.

An investigation is under way to sample lead on farmland, playgrounds and water, while Missouri and Illinois agencies set up extra monitoring.
