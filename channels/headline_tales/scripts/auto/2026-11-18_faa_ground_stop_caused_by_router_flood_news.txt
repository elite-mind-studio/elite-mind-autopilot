The router, being deployed at a site in Arkansas, began sending about one thousand connection attempts every four to five seconds, overwhelming the system and destabilizing aircraft communications and radar feeds.

L3Harris, the contractor that runs the network, struggled for hours to locate the exact IP address, despite a monitoring center in Florida that tracks outages in real time.

The FAA initially flagged two possible sites—one in Amarillo, Texas, and the Arkansas location—before concluding the new router was the cause.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the incident underscores the need for a massive FAA modernization effort, and he is pushing for billions of dollars to replace aging equipment.
