June 1962. Alcatraz. The prison on an island in San Francisco Bay, built so nobody could ever escape.

For six months, three prisoners have been digging. Frank Morris, and brothers John and Clarence Anglin. With spoons from the mess hall, old saw blades, and a drill made from a vacuum cleaner motor.

On the night of the eleventh of June, the guards walk past their cells. Three men asleep in bed.

Except they are not men. They are dummy heads, made from soap, toothpaste, concrete dust and toilet paper.

The real men have climbed through the vents, and paddled into the freezing bay on a raft made of raincoats.

The FBI later said they most likely drowned. But their bodies were never found.

And here is the strange part. The US Marshals still have the case open.
