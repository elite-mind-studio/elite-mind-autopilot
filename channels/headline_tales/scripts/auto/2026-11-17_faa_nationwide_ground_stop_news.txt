Nobody tells you this. Nearly eight thousand flights were cancelled and more than ten thousand delayed after a nationwide ground stop crippled U.S. air traffic.
The FAA halted departures at 20:54 UTC on November thirteenth when a new router flooded the system with a thousand connection attempts every few seconds, choking communication at seventeen control centers.
That four-and-a-half hour freeze forced about three thousand five hundred scheduled flights to stay on the ground, while planes already in the sky were allowed to finish their routes.
Airlines scrambled: United Airlines reported a ninety-minute outage in its weight-and-balance system, delaying seven hundred fifteen flights, though it said there was no sign of a cyberattack.
The ripple reached beyond the U.S.: London-Heathrow saw twenty-nine cancellations and nineteen delays, and Mexican authorities kept U.S.–bound flights on the tarmac until clearance returned.
