A newly installed router flooded the FAA network with a thousand connection attempts every four to five seconds, forcing a rare nationwide ground stop.
The three-and-a-half-hour halt halted departures at every U.S. airport, cancelling nearly three thousand five hundred scheduled flights and .
Passengers were left stranded in packed terminals as gates filled and some inbound planes were diverted to smaller fields.
Officials say the outage stemmed from an upgrade that swapped aging copper lines for fiber at a remote site, exposing a telecom network built on decades-old copper, floppy disks and antique keyboards.
Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the FAA now needs fifty billion dollars from Congress to rebuild the system from the ground up.
