You won't believe this one. The FBI says cargo theft losses in the United States and Canada reached nearly seven hundred twenty-five million dollars last year, a sixty percent rise from the previous year.

In Southern California fourteen men boarded moving freight trains, cut hoses to force stops, and stole more than twenty-two million dollars of merchandise.

They filmed every act, from sawing into containers on the tracks to posing with the loot, and shared the videos on Instagram.

Stolen items—including Nike sneakers, electronics and power tools—were hidden in nearby stash houses and sold on the black market, disrupting supply chains and raising prices for shoppers.

Law enforcement used infrared drones and other tech to track the thieves, leading to arrests and what prosecutors called the granddaddy of train cargo theft cases in Los Angeles County.
