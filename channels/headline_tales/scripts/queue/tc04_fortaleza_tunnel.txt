Have you ever heard of a garden shop that robbed a central bank? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

Fortaleza, Brazil, 2005. A new business opens in a house near the Banco Central. The sign says Grama Sintetica. Synthetic grass.

Every day, workers come and go. Every day, a loaded van drives away. The neighbours think nothing of it. A grass company moves a lot of earth.

But they are not selling grass. For at least three months, they are digging.

The tunnel runs about eighty metres, four metres under a main road. It is lined with plastic, and lit with electric lights.

On the weekend of the sixth of August, 2005, they break through into the vault. They take five containers of fifty real notes. Three and a half tonnes of cash. About one hundred and sixty four million reais, more than seventy million dollars.

On Monday morning, the bank opens the vault. It is almost empty.

But here is the part that ruined everything.

That same weekend, some of the gang go car shopping, and they pay in cash. Lots of cars. Lots of cash. The dealer finds it strange, and he alerts the authorities.

Within days, police stop a truck carrying some of those cars, more than two thousand kilometres away. Hidden inside is one million reais.

Over the years, more than one hundred people were arrested. But only about thirty two million reais was ever recovered. Most of the money was never found.
