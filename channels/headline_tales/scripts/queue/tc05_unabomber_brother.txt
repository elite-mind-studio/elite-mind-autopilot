Have you ever heard of a man the FBI couldn't catch for seventeen years, until his own brother read the newspaper? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

From 1978, bombs start arriving across America. In packages. In the mail. At universities, and on an airline.

The FBI calls the case UNABOM. Over seventeen years, sixteen bombs. Three people killed. Twenty three injured. It becomes the longest and most expensive investigation in FBI history. And still, they have no name.

Then, in 1995, the bomber makes a demand. Publish my manifesto, and I will stop.

On the nineteenth of September, 1995, The Washington Post prints it. Thirty five thousand words.

But here is the part nobody saw coming.

In New York, a man named David Kaczynski reads it. His wife Linda had asked him to. And something feels horribly familiar.

The ideas. The phrases. They sound like the letters his older brother Ted used to write.

David now faces the hardest choice of his life. Protect his brother, or stop the bombs.

He goes to the FBI.

On the third of April, 1996, agents surround a tiny cabin near Lincoln, Montana. Inside, they find bomb parts, and the original manifesto. Ted Kaczynski is arrested.

He pleaded guilty and was sentenced to life in prison without parole. He died in prison in 2023.

The FBI spent seventeen years and more than fifty million dollars. In the end, it was a brother who knew his voice.
