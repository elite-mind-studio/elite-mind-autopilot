June 1990. A British Airways flight climbs out of Birmingham, heading for Spain. Eighty seven people on board.

At seventeen thousand feet, there is a bang. The window in front of the captain blows out.

And the captain, Tim Lancaster, is sucked out of his seat. Headfirst. Halfway out of the plane.

A flight attendant grabs his legs. Then another takes over. They hold on to him for about twenty minutes, in freezing wind, while the first officer flies the plane alone.

But here is the cause. The new window had been fitted with the wrong bolts. Most were slightly too thin.

The first officer lands safely at Southampton.

And the captain? He survived, with frostbite and broken bones. And he went back to flying.
