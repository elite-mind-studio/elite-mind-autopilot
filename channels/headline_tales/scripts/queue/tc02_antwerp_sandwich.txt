Have you ever heard of a hundred million dollar heist that was solved by a half-eaten sandwich? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

Antwerp, Belgium. The diamond capital of the world. Two floors under the Antwerp Diamond Centre sits a vault that was meant to be impossible to rob.

It had a combination lock, infrared heat detectors, a seismic sensor, Doppler radar, and a magnetic field detector.

For more than two years, an Italian thief named Leonardo Notarbartolo rented an office in the building. He looked like any other diamond dealer. He was really studying the vault.

On the weekend of the fifteenth of February, 2003, his crew got in. Diamonds, gold, jewellery and cash were taken. More than one hundred million dollars, by most estimates.

It looked like the perfect crime.

But here is the part nobody planned for.

On the way out, the gang dumped bags of rubbish in a small forest by the motorway. The landowner, August Van Camp, was tired of people dumping on his land. So he called the police.

Inside the bags were envelopes from the Diamond Centre, a videotape, rubber gloves, and a half-eaten salami sandwich. The sandwich carried DNA.

Five days after the heist, Notarbartolo walked back into the Diamond Centre. Detectives were waiting for him.

He was sentenced to ten years in prison. Three of his crew got five years each.

But most of the diamonds have never been found. Somewhere out there, a fortune is still missing.
