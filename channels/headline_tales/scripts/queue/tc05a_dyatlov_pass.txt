February 1959. The Ural Mountains. Nine experienced hikers, led by a student named Igor Dyatlov, set up their tent on a snowy slope.

That night, something made them panic.

They cut their way out of the tent. From the inside. And they ran into the dark, in temperatures far below freezing. Most were not wearing proper clothes. Some had no shoes.

Weeks later, searchers found the first bodies. The last four were found in May, deep under the snow in a ravine. Three of them had terrible injuries.

The Soviet investigators wrote that they died because of a compelling natural force. Nothing more.

For sixty years, people argued. Then in 2020, Russian officials said it was an avalanche. A Swiss study in 2021 showed how a small slab avalanche could explain it.

But some people still do not believe it.
