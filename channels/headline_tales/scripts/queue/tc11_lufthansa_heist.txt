Have you ever heard of the real robbery behind the movie Goodfellas? It was even darker than the film. Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

New York, the eleventh of December, 1978. Around three in the morning. A van pulls up at the Lufthansa cargo building at JFK Airport.

Inside the building, there is a vault. And that night, it is full of cash.

The robbers know exactly where to go. Because they had help from the inside. An airport worker named Louis Werner had told them about the money, in exchange for a cut.

They work fast. When they drive away, they have about five million dollars in cash, plus jewels. Nearly six million dollars. It was the biggest cash robbery in American history at the time.

It should have been the perfect score.

But here is the part that turned it into a nightmare.

The man investigators believed planned it was Jimmy Burke, an associate of a New York crime family. According to investigators and later testimony, he became paranoid that people would talk.

In the months after the heist, several people connected to the robbery were killed, or simply disappeared.

Louis Werner, the inside man, was the only person ever convicted. Burke was never charged for the heist itself.

Years later, the story was told by Henry Hill, a mobster turned informant. It became the heart of the 1990 film Goodfellas.

And the money? It was never recovered.
