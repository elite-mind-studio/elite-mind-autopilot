Have you ever heard of a gang of pensioners who pulled off one of the biggest burglaries in English history? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

London, Easter weekend, 2015. Hatton Garden, the city's jewelry quarter. Underneath one building is a vault full of safe deposit boxes.

The thieves climb down a lift shaft. Then, with a giant industrial drill, they start cutting into the vault wall. It is fifty centimeters of concrete.

Just after midnight on the third of April, the burglar alarm goes off. The police are told. And nobody comes.

The gang drills through. They empty box after box. Gold, jewels, cash. Around fourteen million pounds.

When the vault is opened after the holiday, the whole country is stunned. Who could do this?

But here is the part that shocked everyone.

The men behind it were not young. They were career criminals, most in their sixties and seventies. The leader, Brian Reader, was seventy six years old.

They thought it was their last big job. But cameras had caught their car. Detectives from the Flying Squad began following them, and listening to them talk.

And the old men could not stop talking. About the job. About the money.

Within weeks, they were arrested. In March 2016, the main gang were sentenced to between six and seven years each.

But of the fourteen million pounds, only about four million was ever recovered. The rest is still missing.
