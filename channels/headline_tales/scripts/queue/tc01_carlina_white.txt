Have you ever heard the story of a kidnapped baby who solved her own case? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

New York, the fourth of August, 1987. A baby girl named Carlina White is just nineteen days old. She is in Harlem Hospital with a high fever.

For weeks, a woman dressed as a nurse has been walking the hospital halls. That night, during a shift change, she removes the baby's drip and walks out. Carlina is gone.

Her parents, Joy White and Carl Tyson, search for years. Nothing.

Forty-five miles away, in Bridgeport, Connecticut, a little girl grows up with a different name. Nejdra Nance. Everyone calls her Netty. The woman raising her is Ann Pettway.

But something never feels right. Netty has no birth certificate.

In 2005, she is pregnant and needs it for health insurance. Pettway hands her a document. Officials reject it. It's fake. That evening, Pettway admits she is not her real mother.

But here is the part that will give you chills.

Netty starts searching on her own. At twenty-three, on the missing children website, she finds a photo of a baby taken in 1987. The face looks like her own baby pictures. It looks like her daughter.

In January 2011, a DNA test confirms it. Netty is Carlina White. After twenty-three years, she is back with her real parents.

Ann Pettway pleaded guilty to kidnapping and was sentenced to twelve years in prison.
