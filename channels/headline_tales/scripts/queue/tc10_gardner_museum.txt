Have you ever heard of the biggest art theft in history, where the thieves simply rang the doorbell? Sit tight. Let's go on a journey.

Boston, the early hours of the eighteenth of March, 1990. The city is still waking up from St. Patrick's Day.

Two men in police uniforms arrive at the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum. They tell the guard they are responding to a disturbance. He lets them in.

That was the mistake.

The fake officers overpower the two guards, tie them up, and leave them in the basement. Then, for eighty one minutes, they walk through the museum and take what they want.

Thirteen works of art. A painting by Vermeer called The Concert. Rembrandt's only seascape, The Storm on the Sea of Galilee. Works by Degas and Manet.

Together, they are worth about five hundred million dollars.

But here is the part that makes it so haunting.

The paintings have never been found. Not one.

The FBI believes the thieves were connected to organized crime in Boston. But no one has ever been charged, and the main suspects are now dead.

The museum offers a reward of ten million dollars for information that leads to getting the art back.

And if you visit today, you will see something strange. The empty frames are still hanging on the walls. Waiting for the paintings to come home.

Somewhere out there, half a billion dollars of art is hidden. Someone knows where.
