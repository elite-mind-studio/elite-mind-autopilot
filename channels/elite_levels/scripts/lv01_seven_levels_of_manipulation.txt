There are seven levels of manipulation. Most people only spot the first two. Stay to the end for the one question that protects you from all of them.

Level one. Flattery. "You're the only one who really gets me." You know it's a line, and it still works. In a 2010 study, people who brushed off fake compliments still felt warmer toward the person who gave them.

Level two. The favor you never asked for. In 1971, a researcher's helper bought strangers a soda. Later, those people bought about twice as many raffle tickets from him. A gift creates a debt.

Level three. The small yes. In 1966, most homeowners refused to put a huge safety sign on their lawn. But people who had first agreed to a tiny one mostly said yes.

Level four. The clock. "This offer ends tonight." In a 1975 study, the same cookies seemed better when only two were left in the jar instead of ten.

Level five. The crowd. "Everyone else already agreed." In Solomon Asch's experiments, about three out of four people went along with an obviously wrong group answer at least once.

Level six. Hot and cold. Warm one day, distant the next. Rewards you can't predict are the hardest habit to quit. It's the pull of a slot machine.

Level seven. Rewriting the past. "That never happened. You're too sensitive." Hear it enough, and you trust them more than your own memory. That's gaslighting.

So here's the one question. "If I said no right now, what would happen?" If the honest answer is guilt, punishment, or a sudden coldness, it was never a request. It was pressure.
